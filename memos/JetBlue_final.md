**Re:** Weather shutdown day in New York: over 50% of JetBlue's New York flights cancelled on any day this winter

On February 23, 2026, JetBlue cancelled 304 of its 305 New York flights. A day like that puts an estimated $12.4M of revenue at risk.

**The exposure**
About half of JetBlue's daily flights start or end at a New York-area airport (FY2025 10-K). One shutdown day puts **$12.4M** of revenue at risk ($9,062M revenue × 50% New York share / 365). A cross-check using passengers × average fare gives $11.4M.

JetBlue is already running at a $368M operating loss. With planes 82.4% full, there is also little room to rebook stranded passengers.

**How likely it is**
We estimate **50%**. Across 10 winters of federal BTS flight data (2016-17 to 2025-26), 5 had at least one shutdown day. We excluded March 2020 because it was COVID, not weather. Last winter had two separate shutdowns of 2+ days (Jan 25-26 and Feb 22-24).

**The hedge**
Event contracts pay $1 if the event happens. The price is the probability plus a 2c spread, so $0.52.

| Coverage | Contracts | Cost | Net if event | Net if no event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | 3,103,425 | $1.6M | -$10.9M | -$1.6M |
| 50% | 6,206,850 | $3.2M | -$9.4M | -$3.2M |
| 100% | 12,413,699 | $6.5M | -$6.5M | -$6.5M |

Unhedged, the loss is $12.4M if the event happens and nothing if it doesn't.

**Suggested starting point: 25% coverage.** It cuts the loss to $10.9M if a shutdown day happens, and the premium is the smallest of the three. At a 50% probability, the hedge is pricey, so larger coverage buys more certainty at a higher cost.

**What this doesn't cover**
- Actual losses may differ from $12.4M. Some revenue comes back through rebooking and higher fares on remaining seats, while storm costs (de-icing, crews, hotels) push the other way. The contract pays on the event, not on your actual loss.
- A cheaper alternative is to cover only 2+ day storms, which we estimate at 10%. That would protect against the worst case but not a single-day shutdown.
- The contract pays once per winter. A season with several shutdown days, like 2025–26 with five, would leave the extra days unhedged.
